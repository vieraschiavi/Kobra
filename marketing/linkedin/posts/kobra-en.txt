A scoring model with 0.87 AUC can be worse than a rule of thumb.

Seven questions to find that out BEFORE you sign. Save these — none of them require knowing how to code.

1. Is the validation temporal or random?
If they shuffled past and future when splitting the data, the model already saw what it's supposed to predict. In collections, fraud and churn, time order IS the signal.

2. What's the selection-to-holdout gap?
They tried 40 configurations and reported the best one. That slice was already spent choosing. The honest number is how far it falls on a slice they never touched. If they don't show it, they didn't measure it.

3. Compared to what?
An AUC of 0.84 alone says nothing. What does the rule the business uses TODAY score? If the model beats it by 0.01, the project doesn't pay for itself.

4. KS, or only AUC?
AUC averages the whole range. KS tells you whether it separates at the cutoff you'll actually use — the only one that matters.

5. Is there a variable you won't have on decision day?
The most common leak isn't bad faith: it's a column filled in AFTER the event. In the history it's there. In production, when you have to decide, it isn't yet.

6. What happens when the population shifts?
Every model degrades. The question isn't whether — it's how often they retrain, and who tells you it dropped.

7. Can you explain a single decision?
If the system deprioritized a case and nobody can say why, you can't defend it to a customer or to a regulator.

I'm building a collections platform where all seven are answered on screen, unprompted. Including #2, the uncomfortable one: the AutoML gap is +0.0729 and it's visible, not buried.

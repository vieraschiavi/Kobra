Your collections team calls between 9 and 6.

Your debtor also works between 9 and 6.

That alone explains a huge share of the calls nobody picks up. But it isn't the most expensive thing happening inside a collections operation.

The most expensive thing is the ORDER.

Almost every team calls highest balance first. It sounds logical: go for the big one. The problem is that the balance tells you nothing about whether that money is coming back.

A $50,000 debt from someone who won't pay is worth less, in practice, than three $5,000 debts from people who will.

Sorting by balance is sorting by what you WANT to collect.
Sorting by balance × probability of payment is sorting by what you WILL collect.

Those are two different lists. And you can build the second one tomorrow, with a spreadsheet and the history you already have: check what share of similar cases ended up paying, multiply, re-sort.

Three more things that cost nothing and almost nobody does:

→ Call in the time window where that person actually answered before, not the one that suits the call center.

→ Stop calling someone who already committed to pay on the 5th. You're burning money, and every extra call makes them less likely to pick up next time.

→ Count how many attempts each case takes. Past four, the channel is wrong. It isn't that the person won't talk — it's that they won't talk THERE.

None of this requires buying software. It requires looking at two columns you already have in your system.

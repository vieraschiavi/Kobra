If a machine learning product won't show you its selection-to-holdout gap, it isn't showing you performance. It's showing you its best angle.

The trap is old and still works: you train, you try a few configurations, you pick the one that scored best, and you report THAT number. But you already spent that slice choosing. It isn't a test — it's a mirror.

The honest number is the other one: how far the model falls on a slice you never touched.

I recorded the platform I'm building showing exactly that, on screen, unprompted:

· Walk-forward AUC: 0.8381
· KS: 0.5662
· Against the rule the business uses today: +0.2134
· AutoML selection→holdout gap: +0.0729

Temporal validation: 75% past to train, 25% future to evaluate. Never random — in collections, time order IS the signal.

That +0.0729 is the uncomfortable number. We leave it visible anyway, because a model that hides its own drop is a model you can't put money decisions on.
